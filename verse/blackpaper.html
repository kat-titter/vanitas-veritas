vanitas_veritas

black paper, october 2026

This is the context for vanitas_veritas, a work in a sink by Kat Titterton. It stands in two places with one water: vanitas, the sink, a stone basin in a room, proposed; and veritas, the same basin on the web. A white paper, in black.

In short. A sink you cannot wash your hands in. The water is a machine dreaming: a GPU writes a new vanitas prompt, dreams it, and walks to the next, frame by frame, without end, and the frames pour from the tap to the drain. Reach in, and the dream goes where your hand goes for a moment, then slips through your fingers, down the drain. Switch the GPU off and the water stops. No water, no dream. A touch in vanitas moves the water in veritas, and a touch in veritas moves the water in vanitas. At vanitas, if you ask it to, a camera gives your face to the next dream.

01  the thesis

no water, no dream.

Machines that dream at scale drink water: data centres evaporate it to stay cool, and one estimate puts the training of a single large language model at 700,000 litres of fresh water1. Here the metaphor is plumbing. The water in the basin is a GPU dreaming. While it dreams, the tap runs. Switch it off, and what is left runs to the drain and the basin stays dry.

why a sink.

Slop is everything but the kitchen sink, so the work brings the sink. The building was ready for it: under tiat’s gallery on Powell Street, where the work was first proposed, the basement keeps “a slop station (an empty room with one metal sink)”2, found only after the sink was drawn. And San Francisco’s tech has carried a sink before. On 26 October 2022, Elon Musk walked into Twitter’s headquarters with one in his arms, “let that sink in”, as his purchase of the company, now X, neared its close3. A sink is where tech makes a joke of itself. This one does not let you wash your hands of it.

you can't wash your hands of it.

Vanitas still lifes, from Leiden in the early 1600s, pile up skulls, cut flowers and soap bubbles to say that everything you want is already going4. This one never holds still long enough to want. Each dream is new, never repeated, and on its way down the drain. Reach in, and the water under your hand goes where your hand goes; then it slips through your fingers. A sink you cannot wash your hands in, placed where people wash their hands, says the rest without a word.

sink == sync

In 1665 Christiaan Huygens saw two clocks hung from one beam fall into a shared rhythm5. Here two basins hang from one spring. vanitas and veritas drink the same dream at the same moment, and a hand in either moves the water in both. Their names are one swap of letters apart6, as sink and sync are one sound apart: one water.

water = gpu.dream() # no gpu, no water

02  veritas

veritas is one web page, sync.html, open to anyone: a basin seen from above, a rectangle like the frames it carries, under ribbed glass, with a tap at the top and a drain in the middle. It explains nothing about itself. Six things, for the record:

the spring
A GPU that dreams without end. Each dream begins as a new prompt in the grammar of the Midjourney recipe Kat’s stills came from, vanitas [flower, skull, soap bubble]: three of the old symbols and a way of going, never the same words twice: 1.3 million prompts, about five years of dreaming. It is dreamed out of the last dream, pulled back toward one of the eight pictures on her vanitas moodboard so the taste stays hers, with open-source diffusion7 made fast enough for a small computer8, and graded silver and amber like the sources. On the way to the next dream it dreams three more places and glides through all of them, so forms melt into one another instead of fading. Every frame is drawn at 2560 by 1920: on Kat’s Mac, a frame every two to three seconds. A picture Kat makes by hand in Midjourney and saves to the spring’s folder becomes its next dream.
the water
The frames pour in at the tap and run to the drain, carried by a stable-fluids solver9 with a vortex at the hole. Every half minute or so a dream comes into focus; then the water takes it apart again. Besides its name, the only words on the page are code: the prompt being dreamed, water = dream("vanitas [peony, skull, soap bubble], blooming"), and the grip while you hold.
the hand
Press, and the water under your palm goes where your hand goes. The glass lies flat over it, and the moment you reached in stops changing. The grip loosens by the second, grip = 0.55 after one, the water leaks out between the fingers, and what you held slips down the drain. The space bar is the plug: the drain closes and the dream pools, and when it opens, everything goes.
no water
When the spring stops, the tap closes; what is left runs to the drain, and the line reads water = None. A last drop, now and then. Without WebGL2, the page shows an empty basin.
your face
Only at vanitas, and only if you ask. Touching the water never wakes the camera: it has its own place on the glass, an eye. Hold it for two seconds; a ring fills, one line says what will happen, and letting go stops it. Then the camera opens for under a second, and that one frame of your face goes to the spring as the reference for the next dream, the way Midjourney’s omni reference takes a face. The line of code says so: --oref you. A few seconds later the water is dreaming you, in every basin at once, and you go down the drain too.
one water
A hand in the water travels to every other basin as an anonymous event over public Nostr relays, and is replayed there a moment late: a push, a grip, a thin ring of light. Gold, if the hand is at vanitas.
under the glass

One HTML page: no framework, no build step, no accounts. The solver runs on the visitor’s own graphics card: the frames pour in as the pigment, re-forming out of the newest frame, freshest where the tap lands. A grip pulls the water under the palm toward the hand’s own motion, except in five gaps where the fingers part. The spring is a short Python script around Stable Diffusion 1.5 with LCM-LoRA7,8: a dream takes about four seconds on an Apple M5, and while the GPU dreams on, the chip’s Neural Engine enlarges each frame four times with Real-ESRGAN, with a half-size copy for phones. It writes the newest frame and one small file saying what it is; veritas asks for that file every second or so, and closes the tap when nothing new has come for twenty seconds.

while held: grip *= .55 # it slips

03  vanitas

veritas is the invitation; vanitas is the place. Proposed: a basalt column with the basin as its screen, under ribbed glass. The glass is a touch screen, so a hand on it is a hand in the water, here and in veritas wherever it is open. Where a tap would stand, a small camera faces whoever is there; it wakes only when someone holds its eye on the glass, and gives that face to the next dream. The lever is the tap: push it down and generation stops, no data flows, and the water runs out in both; lift it, and it floods back. A QR code cut into its top opens veritas, so the water follows you out of the room. In full: vanitas.

Status: proposed, not yet built. vanitas was first designed in 2026 for tiat’s Epistemologies of Slop open call in San Francisco6, as a steel utility sink with a self-portrait above it; that design is set aside for this one.

how it works, and what is proposed

Behind the glass, a panel and a small computer run the same page as veritas, with touch through the glass. The camera’s frame goes to the spring with a key only vanitas holds, is used once in memory, and is never written down or compared with anyone. The camera keeps to what data-protection regulators ask of a camera in a public room: a sign at eye level by the door and beside the sink, read before the lens can see you; a gesture of its own, apart from touching the water; a light that shows when it looks; and the whole piece open to anyone who never wakes it. Proposed with it: a few seconds in which a dream made from your face stays in the sink, where you can wash it away before it goes to every basin, and a contact to take it down after. Proposed, and the strongest form: a GPU in the plinth runs the spring, so vanitas is the spring, and the lever is the GPU’s own switch. Until vanitas stands, the spring runs on Kat’s Mac.

Texts, formats and a technical rider for hosts and juries are in the pitch kit.

04  what it keeps

Almost nothing. A hand in the water travels while it moves: where in the basin, whether it grips, and whether it is at vanitas. The relays pass it on and keep nothing. There is no record of a play and no account. At vanitas, one frame of a face goes to the spring and is used once, in memory; the dream made from it goes to every basin, like any other. The spring keeps its last sixty frames on its own disk, deleting the oldest as it goes, and remembers only how far through its prompts it has dreamed.

The page’s host, the relays, the spring’s address and the server the page loads its relay library from are run by others, or by Kat. Like any server a browser reaches, they see a connection’s network address; the events themselves carry none.

05  lineage

  • The genre. Vanitas: the skull, the cut flower, the soap bubble, all of it already going4.
  • The loop. Vanitas Veritas (2026): eight pictures grown from Kat’s self-portrait style code, melted into one another with open-source diffusion, an eight-and-a-half-minute loop with no first frame and no last6. The spring is that loop, opened: it no longer comes back.
  • The web pieces. Three earlier basins, i, ii and iii, September and October 2026, now retired. veritas is the fourth.
the science underneath

Clocks that fall into step on a shared beam5; water that a computer can push around in real time without blowing up9; pictures pulled out of noise in a few steps7,8. The eye behind the piece is a neuroscientist’s: things watched under glass, never quite held.

06  where it goes

A first sink in San Francisco, among the people who build AI. Proposed. veritas can be anywhere; vanitas needs a room, and the first room should be where the dreaming machines are made.

Then wherever an open call takes it. These fit it, all public:

  • Black Rock City. The BRC Honoraria fund interactive, sculptural work on the open playa; the letter of intent for 2027 is due 29 October 2026, 5 pm PDT10. A sink with no water, in a desert.
  • Gray Area, San Francisco. The Cultural Incubator: four months of critiques, a mentor and a public showcase, for individuals and small collectives in the Bay Area, in four tracks; this piece is Generative Media and Synthetic Realities at once11. And its exhibition open calls, as they come.
  • Stochastic Labs, Berkeley. A summer residency for artists, engineers and scientists; in 2026 its theme was liveness and agency, with groups for artificial life and real-time AI. Its call has come each spring12.
  • Kala Art Institute, Berkeley. The Kala Fellowship: a residency for artists working in and across media, with Kala’s facilities and a group show in the Kala Gallery at the end13.
  • Prix Ars Electronica, Linz. Free to enter, online, with categories for animation and interactive art; its calls have opened in January and closed in early March14.

08  who is behind it

macrofluidic is the name Kat Titterton signs her code-poems with; one was signed macrofluidic × midjourney. This one is signed with every hand in the water.

Kat Titterton, San Francisco

Generative artist: Bare Metal, a solo generative-art exhibit at Human Tech Week, San Francisco, 202517; Vanitas Veritas, 20266; code-poems as macrofluidic. Also a scientist in imaging, signal and machine learning, trained in cellular neuroscience and mathematical systems biology: expansion microscopy at MIT’s Synthetic Neurobiology Group; the Cell Painting screens behind two of the largest such datasets, at Axiom Bio; tau-aggregation screens and microfluidic neuron-culture devices at AbbVie; eight peer-reviewed papers17. A freediver: she knows what a breath of water costs.

The work: veritas, and vanitas, proposed. For hosts and juries: the pitch kit. Contact: macrofluidic.me.
