01  the form

drag to turn

a basalt column, as it cooled and fractured. seven faces, left as they came out of the ground, about 57 cm across.

88 cm to the rim. the top is the one worked face: sawn flat, honed matte, never polished.

in the top, the basin: a rectangle 32 by 24 cm, the shape of the dreams, its corners eased. veritas fills it, under glass set flush with the top, and the glass answers a touch. in the middle, a drain.

at the back, where a tap would stand, a brass lever. raw brass, so it darkens where hands hold it. in its hub, a small lens looking back at you.

nothing else but a small square cut into the top at the front: a code, grey in grey, that opens veritas on a phone. no label, no bezel, no cable in sight, no legs. the screen and the computer are inside the column.

the column floats 3 cm over a round black steel plinth set about 6 cm inside its faces. a warm hairline of light on the floor is the only trace of a fixture.

where the form comes from
  • a stone basin before the tea house. you crouch, and the bowl’s mouth is the message: at ryōan-ji its square opening completes four characters read as “i only know plenty”. tsukubai, checked 25 october 2026.
  • basalt cut by machine, set with brass. formafantasma, de natura fossilium, milan, april 2014.
  • a stone column with a stone control, after the baptismal font. giorgio rava, anima liquida, 2021.
  • furniture as one brutal, elegant block of stone. rick owens: furniture, moca, 2016 to 2017.
  • an aperture with light behind it and no fixture in sight. james turrell, as seen below, his hundredth skyspace, aarhus, june 2026.
  • a ship of one material, no gadgets, no buttons, a shaft like a well. arrival, 2016, production design by patrice vermette.
  • the slab painted black after the glassy prop disappointed kubrick. the monolith, 2001.

the lesson across them: the opposite of toy-like is not shiny. it is heavy, quiet, and does one thing.

one = plane + hole + touch # the rest is rock

02  at vanitas

  1. you come up to it. the water is already running: a dream turning toward the drain, never the same twice.
  2. you touch the glass. the water under your hand comes with you, then slips through your fingers, down the drain. in veritas, wherever it is open, a gold ring opens where you touched, and the water there moves too.
  3. touching the water never wakes the lens. to be dreamed, hold the eye on the glass for two seconds: a ring fills, one line says what will happen, and letting go stops it. then the lens in the hub takes one picture of your face for the next dream, and a light shows while it looks. seconds later the water is dreaming you, in every basin, and you go down the drain as well. the picture is never kept; a sign at eye level says so before the lens can see you.
  4. a hand in veritas, anywhere, is a pale ring in this basin, and the water here moves with it.
  5. push the lever down and the tap closes: generation stops, no data flows, and the water runs out here and in veritas. lift it, and it floods back.
  6. on the way out, the code at the front: the same water, on your phone.

veritas invites. vanitas is the place.

04  drawings

for whoever builds it.

stone and glass brass, light, and the dimensions, in mm hidden below
the full spec
form
a vertical prism, as the rock fractured: a seven-sided plan inside a 60 cm circle, faces as quarried, grey where cut. the column is cut once, 12 cm from the top. the cap drops over a steel cradle and the two read as one stone with a hairline seam.
the basin
a 24 in open-frame panel lies flat under the cap, 10 mm below the glass, with a touch film that reads a hand through it. the hole shows 32 by 24 cm of it and the stone masks the rest; veritas, in its vanitas mode, fills the hole edge to edge. the glass is 34 by 26 cm, 8 mm, low-iron so the black stays black, tempered, polished edge, micro-ribbed along the lever’s axis so the ribs sit in step with the page’s own. it sits flush in a rebate on black silicone. cleaning is one wipe.
the tap
a brass bar, 22 mm across and 28 cm long, rising from a 6 cm brass hub set flush in the top, 7 cm in from the back rim. up, the water runs; push it down 20 degrees toward you and it stays there, and generation stops. a magnet in the hub passes a sensor. a tap that turns off a dream: the whole piece in one gesture.
the lens
a small camera behind a 6 mm window in the hub, facing whoever stands at the basin. it wakes only when a hand holds its eye on the glass for two seconds, takes one frame while a light beside it shows, and sleeps.
materials
basalt, one natural column, top honed to 400 grit. raw brass for whatever a hand touches, and for the lens’s ring. blackened steel, waxed, for the plinth, the cradle and the drawer, all in shadow. low-iron micro-ribbed tempered glass. a matte open-frame panel with a touch film. a small fanless computer. no acrylic anywhere.
light
the basin is the only light on the object. the panel sits 10 mm under the glass, capped near 250 nit, so ink stays ink in a dark room; the glass edge catches it and the honed top shows a faint glow round the hole. a 2200 K line in the shadow gap faces the floor and draws the gold hairline. the room at 5 to 10 lux, dark walls and floor where the venue allows. nothing is aimed at the piece. when someone touches, here or anywhere, only the water changes.
the cable
one mains lead leaves the back of the plinth at floor level, under a cord cover painted the floor colour, to one outlet. no plumbing, no wall.
inside vanitas
  • a small fanless computer in the plinth’s drawer, running veritas as sync.html?spring=…&sink&camera&key=…, full screen in a kiosk browser from power-on, drinking from the spring. no keyboard, no mouse, the cursor hidden. proposed: a GPU here, so vanitas is the spring.
  • a flat screen, open frame, matte, about 24 in, lying under the cap on a steel cradle, with its driver board beside it and a touch film under the glass. brightness capped near 250 nit.
  • a small camera in the hub, on one cable down the bore.
  • one cable from the computer up the bore to the screen.
  • a small magnet in the lever’s hub and a hall sensor in a pocket beside it. no contact, no bounce, nothing to wear.
  • a sensor board that acts as a keyboard. while the lever is down it holds the space key, which is the tap in veritas, in its vanitas mode; lift it and it releases.
  • one power supply in the drawer. one mains lead out of the back at floor level.
  • a warm led line in the shadow gap, with its driver, in the plinth.
  • a sachet of silica gel in the sealed bay under the glass.

if the sensor fails, the water keeps running. if the spring stops, the basin drains: that is the piece, not a fault.

05  to stand it

  • a room you can darken to 5 to 10 lux, with nothing aimed at vanitas.
  • a ground floor: about 270 kg, about 2 t per m² on the plinth. an upper floor needs a check.
  • one outlet. wifi or ethernet for the spring and the shared hands; without it the water stops, unless the spring is inside it.
  • no staff. it starts itself and comes back after a power cut. to test before any room: two days running, the power cut ten times.
  • it keeps nothing and asks no one’s name. a face is given only by holding the camera’s eye, used once, for one dream, and never written down; a sign at eye level by the door and beside the sink says so, and the whole piece works for anyone who never wakes the camera.
  • riggers with a pallet jack to place it. two people with suction cups lift the cap for service.
  • about a season from the first order, if the shops are free. a guess.

who makes what
  1. a columnar-basalt yard in the pacific northwest supplies the column cut to length and ships it by freight. if it will, the horizontal cut and the bore too.
  2. a bay area stone yard with a bridge saw and a core rig does the rest of the stone work: the cut, the bore, the cap pocket, the hole and its rebate, the hub pocket, the honed top.
  3. a metal shop makes the plinth with its drawer, the cradle, the brass hub with its lens window and bar, and drills the sensor pocket to a template. blackened and waxed.
  4. a glass shop cuts, tempers and edge-polishes the low-iron ribbed glass.
  5. kat does the panel, its board and touch film, the camera, the computer, the sensor board, veritas in its vanitas mode, the spring, and the burn-in.
