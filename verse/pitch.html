vanitas_veritas

For open calls, juries and hosts. vanitas_veritas is by Kat Titterton: a work in a sink. It stands in two places with one water: vanitas, a stainless sink, designed and not yet built, and veritas, the same basin on the web, built. The context is in the black paper.

statement, about 200 wordsVanitas paintings piled up beautiful things to say they were already going. My pictures are AI bubbles, and I mean it twice: the soap bubble the Dutch painted for fragility, and the speculative one many of us fear we are standing in. Slop is everything but the kitchen sink, so I am bringing the sink: vanitas, a stainless sink with my self-portrait above it. Its water is a GPU dreaming: a new vanitas prompt in the grammar of my Midjourney recipe, dreamed, then walked into the next, never the same twice, and every dream runs to the drain. Machines that dream drink water; here the metaphor is plumbing. The faucet handle is the tap. Hold it and generation stops, no data flows: no water, no dream. Reach in, and the water under your hand comes with you for a moment, then slips through your fingers. I freedive; I know what a held breath costs, and that you cannot hold water. In vanitas, a small camera gives your face to the next dream, once, and lets it go down the drain with the rest. The same water runs on the web, as veritas, one swap of letters away. Huygens saw two clocks on one beam fall into step; here two basins hang from one spring, and a hand in either moves both.

02  the piece

what it is
no water, no dreamMachines that dream drink water; one estimate puts the training of a single large language model at 700,000 litres1. Here the water in the basin is a GPU dreaming. While it dreams, the tap runs.
why a sinkSlop is everything but the kitchen sink. Under tiat’s gallery in San Francisco, the basement keeps “a slop station (an empty room with one metal sink)”2. On 26 October 2022, Elon Musk carried a sink into Twitter’s headquarters: “let that sink in”3.
the springThe GPU. It writes a new prompt in the grammar of Kat’s Midjourney recipe, vanitas [flower, skull, soap bubble], never repeating for 1.3 million prompts, about five years; dreams it with Stable Diffusion 1.5 and LCM-LoRA45, pulled back toward her vanitas moodboard; and dreams three more places on the way to the next, gliding through them. Every frame is 2560 by 1920; on one Mac, a frame every two to three seconds.
the waterThe frames pour from the tap to the drain, carried by a stable-fluids solver6, in a 4:3 basin with rounded corners, like the frames. Besides its name, the only words are code: the prompt, water = dream("vanitas [peony, skull, soap bubble], blooming"), and the grip while you hold.
the handPress, and the water under your hand moves with it, then slips through your fingers, down the drain: grip = 0.55 after one second. In veritas, the space bar is the plug.
no waterWhen the spring stops, the tap closes and the basin drains: water = None.
your facevanitas only, and only if asked: touching the water never wakes the camera, which has its own eye on the glass. Held for two seconds, it takes one frame of a face and sends it to the spring as the reference for the next dream, the open-model version of Midjourney’s omni reference; the line of code then ends --oref you. The frame is used once, in memory, never written down. A sign at eye level says so before the lens can see anyone, and a light shows when it looks.
one waterA touch in any basin travels as an anonymous, ephemeral event over public Nostr relays and moves the water in every other basin; gold, if it comes from vanitas. sink == sync: two basins on one spring, like Huygens’ clocks on one beam7. vanitas and veritas are one swap of letters apart, as sink and sync are one sound apart: one water.

03  now and next

One work in two places, one water. veritas is the invitation; vanitas is the place; a hand in either moves the water in both.

whenwhat
veritasnow, builtveritas and the spring are built and run together on Kat’s Mac. veritas is public at its own address, which finds the spring by itself whenever the spring runs.
vanitas2027, designedA freestanding stainless sink with a self-portrait above it, one water with veritas: designed, priced and not yet built. First call: CURRENTS 2027, Santa Fe. No room is confirmed.
a second sinklater, proposedAnother sink in another city, the same water.

Each also shows alone: veritas online or on a wall, vanitas in an exhibition.

06  bio

Kat Titterton, about 80 wordsKat Titterton is a San Francisco artist and imaging scientist. She ran and tuned expansion-microscopy pipelines at MIT, designed microfluidic neuron cultures at AbbVie, and oversaw the Cell Painting screens behind two of the largest such datasets at Axiom Bio. As macrofluidic she makes generative work about what images do once machines make them: Bare Metal (Human Tech Week, San Francisco, 2025) and vanitas_veritas (2026, ongoing). She freedives, and knows what a breath of water costs.
