I'm Kat (macrofluidic, @KatTitterton), SF neuroscience/AI scientist and generative artist. I'm making **vanitas_veritas**, a work in a sink. It stands in two places with one water: **vanitas**, the sink, a physical stone basin (proposed, first for San Francisco; its doc is `sink.html`), and **veritas**, the same basin on the web (`sync.html`). The two names are one swap of letters apart, as sink and sync are one sound apart: one water. The water is a GPU dreaming: **the spring** (`code/spring.py`) dreams vanitas still lifes without end, and the frames pour from the tap to the drain. No GPU, no water, no dream. Nothing physical is built or confirmed. vanitas began as the stainless-steel sink I designed in 2026 for tiat's Epistemologies of Slop open call (its docs are in `documents/`; the Submission and Web docs carry the water since 2026-10-25). The work is mine alone.

**Repo:** `/Users/kat_titter/Claude/Projects/tiat_slop_2026` (GitHub `kat-titter/vanitas-veritas`; the built pages publish to kat-titter.github.io/vanitas_veritas_verse/ with `deploy_verse.py`, which also publishes `sync.html` and `sink.html`; i and ii stay at kat-titter.github.io/vanitas-veritas/verse/ once this folder is pushed, and that folder's `index.html` forwards to the new home). The web pages live in `verse/`, the spring in `code/`.

- `code/spring.py` is the spring: Stable Diffusion 1.5 + LCM-LoRA on my Mac (MPS) for now. Each dream is a new prompt in the grammar of my Midjourney recipe `vanitas [flower, skull, soap bubble]`, never repeating for 1.3 million prompts, about five years, dreamed out of the last dream and pulled back toward my vanitas moodboard (`source/moodboard/`, eight pictures; `source/veritas/` if it is missing), graded with `harmonize.py`. On the way to the next dream it dreams three waypoints and glides through all of them on one Catmull-Rom path (48 frames a dream, about two minutes; each dream has its own noise, and its three waypoints share it). The dreaming runs in fp16 on the GPU; Real-ESRGAN x4 runs on the Neural Engine in a process of its own (`code/upscale.py`), so every frame is 2560x1920 with a 1280x960 copy, one every two to three seconds. A picture I make by hand in Midjourney and save to `source/mj_inbox/` becomes the next dream once, its words read from the picture's own metadata; nothing calls Midjourney. It also serves veritas, and only veritas, `now.json` and the frames (never the key or `state.json`). Run `caffeinate -i python3 code/spring.py`, then open http://127.0.0.1:8770/ (it redirects to `sync.html?spring`); `--public` also opens a Cloudflare quick tunnel from my Mac and prints the public address (it changes each run; `cloudflared` is in `~/.local/bin/`). `--sheet 8` dreams eight on one contact sheet with their light and warmth and stops; `--dry` serves veritas with no GPU (the water off); `--lan` lets vanitas drink from the same network. The code it shares with the render is in `code/latent.py`; old experiments are in `code/archive/`.
- `spring_feed/` (outside git, gitignored): the last sixty frames, `now.json` (the newest frame, its prompt, whether the tap is open), `state.json` (how far through the prompts), and `key`, which only vanitas holds, for the camera and the tap.
- `verse/sync.html` is veritas: one page, WebGL2, its title `veritas` all the time, a 4:3 basin with rounded corners like the frames; a stable-fluids solver pours the newest frame from the tap to the drain. Press: the water under your hand moves with it, then slips away (`grip = 0.55` after one second). Space is the plug. When nothing new has come for twenty seconds the tap closes and the basin drains (`water = None`). Hands travel between basins as ephemeral Nostr events (kind 20777, tag `veritas-basin`; damus, nos.lol, primal; nostr-tools 2.25.2 from esm.sh); `?solo` turns that off. `SPRING_HOME` in the page is the spring's lasting address, empty until it has one, so on the published site the water is off; the spring serves the page itself, so its tunnel address is veritas, live.
- Inside vanitas: `sync.html?spring=<feed>&sink&camera&key=<spring_feed/key>`. The basin fills the screen. Touching the water never opens the camera: it has its own eye on the glass (a ring at the bottom, shown only here). Held for two seconds (the ring fills and one line says what will happen; letting go stops it), the camera opens for under a second (at most every 20 s), the ring glows while it looks, and one frame goes with the key to the spring's `/feed/face`, as the omni reference for the next dream (the prompt line ends `--oref you`); the spring keeps it in memory, uses it once and never writes it. Space is the lever, which is the tap (`/feed/tap`): down, the spring rests and every basin drains; up, it floods back. A hand on vanitas shows as a gold ring in every other basin.

**Slow, live, labelled (2026-10-25, last):** change in the basin is now just perceptible (my words: you have to get close to notice). The spring walks 48 frames a dream, one every 2.5 s, so about two minutes from dream to dream (it was 16 frames, about 45 s; the frames changed by 22 of 255 levels across 12 s). veritas's water is about three times calmer (flow, drain vortex, tap eddies) and its breath comes every 60 s instead of 26. The reason: changes slow enough draw no attention, and even large ones go unnoticed (Simons, Franconeri & Reimer 2000, *Perception* 29(10), 1143 to 1154, doi:10.1068/p3104). **The spring rebuilt, public, and the camera's consent (2026-10-25, late):** this overrides the numbers and the camera in every entry below.
- The walk: dreamed waypoints and a Catmull-Rom path, judged against the old random-detour slerp on the same two dreams (three judges preferred the new one: 7 to 4, 6 to 5, 7 to 5). One noise for many dreams, as the v44 loop had, was tried and dropped: it imprints one picture (the same dots in the same places) on every dream. - Measured on my M5 (16 GB): a dream about 4 s, a waypoint about 4 s, a decode about 1.5 to 2 s with the upscaler busy beside it, Real-ESRGAN x4 0.76 s a frame on the Neural Engine (2.36 s on the GPU); live, 2.0 to 2.8 s of GPU time a frame across tonight's runs and a frame poured every 2.2 to 2.9 s; the spring holds 2.3 GB of GPU memory. Two traps found: Core ML holds Python's lock while it runs (so the upscaler has its own process), and attention slicing turns fp16 dreams to NaN on the Mac's GPU (so it is off, and an empty dream raises instead of pouring black).

- Consent, from what established works and regulators do (researched 2026-10-25; not legal advice): there is no shared standard among artists (Beeple's REGULAR ANIMALS at the Neue Nationalgalerie posts a GDPR notice and deletes raw images daily; ENESS at Kalmar Slott says no images are saved; teamLab's policy never mentions cameras). Regulators agree on: notice at eye level before the lens can see you (EDPB Guidelines 3/2019, paras 113 to 115, https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_201903_video_devices_en_0.pdf; ICO, https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/cctv-and-video-surveillance/), no surprising or unexpected collection (FTC policy statement on biometric information, 18 May 2023, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/05/ftc-warns-about-misuses-biometric-information-harm-consumers), and nobody losing out for saying no. Face detection with no stored template sits on the safer side of Illinois BIPA (Zellmer v. Meta, 9th Cir. 2024), CCPA reaches only businesses over its thresholds, and San Francisco's ban on face recognition (Admin. Code ch. 19B) binds City departments, not private parties.
