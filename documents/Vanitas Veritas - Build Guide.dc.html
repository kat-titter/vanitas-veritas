how it fits together
One computer does everything: it dreams the water, makes it sharp, and serves the page the screen shows. Every cable runs down through the drain to it, so nothing shows above the bowl.
wiring
side section
Its faucet handle is the GPU’s power switch. Held, it sends Space: the spring rests and every basin, here and on the web, drains; let go and it floods back. Its power strip is the whole piece’s. Off, nothing runs; on, everything comes back by itself. Touching the screen moves the water here and in every other basin.

the computer: three commands
About 30 minutes, most of it downloading. Do it at home on a monitor, then move the Mac mini into the sink. Every step can be run again safely.
1
Prepare the Mac10 min
Update macOS. In System Settings: Users & Groups, log in automatically; Energy, prevent sleep and start up after a power failure; Notifications, Focus on. Install Google Chrome into Applications.
2
Get the piece1 min
Open Terminal and paste:
git clone https://github.com/kat-titter/vanitas-veritas.git ~/vanitas-veritas
3
Install and test25 min
Installs Python packages, the models and the moodboard, then dreams two frames. Ending on a contact sheet means it works.
cd ~/vanitas-veritas && bash code/setup_sink.sh
4
Make it run for good2 min
Starts the spring and the full-screen page now and at every login, and restarts either if it stops.
bash code/sink.sh install
5
Check it1 min
Within about a minute, the screen shows the water. Anytime:
bash code/sink.sh status
good to know
Add a Midjourney picture by dropping it into source/mj_inbox/: it becomes the next dream. Quit the kiosk with Command-Q, or stop everything with bash code/sink.sh remove. Its public address is printed in the log, ~/Library/Logs/vanitas/.

the sink: one afternoon
Assemble with the power off. Nothing is glued that might need to come out.
1
Stand the sink30 min
Assemble the legs, level it, and set the Mac mini on a shelf or bracket under the bowl, clear of the floor.
2
Run the cables15 min
Remove the drain basket. Feed HDMI, the screen’s USB and its power up through the drain hole; leave slack below.
3
Bed the screen45 min
Four foam blocks on the bowl floor; the screen face up on them, level, its top just below the rim. Connect the cables, then lay the black frame on top, opening centred.
4
Fit the handle switch30 min
Set the foot switch to send Space (its own app, on any computer). Fix its pedal under the faucet handle so a full pull presses it, and put the power symbol on the handle (a small vinyl decal). Test in TextEdit: a pull types a space.
5
Mount the print and camera45 min
Bolt the post behind the sink, hang the print, clip the camera to its top edge, aimed at where a face would be.
6
Five-minute test5 min
Power on: the water appears within two minutes. Touch: it follows your finger. Pull the handle: it drains; let go: it floods back. Hold the ring on the glass two seconds: the camera light shows once. Pull the plug and replug: it comes back on its own.
if something is wrong
screen black, says water = Nonethe spring is starting or stopped: wait two minutes, then bash code/sink.sh restart
touch does nothingthe screen’s USB cable: reseat it at the Mac mini
handle does nothingthe switch must send Space: test it in TextEdit
frames slow or stutterthe Mac mini is too hot: check the fan and the gap under the bowl
no public addressthe internet is down; the sink still works on its own

keep it running
Built to be left alone for the whole show.
1
Burn in2 days
Before the show, run it 48 hours, then pull the plug ten times. Every time it must come back to the water on its own.
2
Each morning
Nothing to do. Power on, and the Mac mini starts; the screen follows the spring.
3
Each night
Switch off at the power strip, or leave it on: the spring keeps only the last 60 frames on disk.
4
Update2 min
When the code changes:
cd ~/vanitas-veritas && git pull && bash code/sink.sh restart
5
Remote check
From any browser, the public address in the log shows the same water. If it moves, the sink is alive.

why it is built this way
Four choices keep it simple to run.
One computer, no cloud
Dreaming, upscaling and serving run on the Mac mini. No account, no subscription. Two switches: the faucet handle rests the GPU while it is held; the power strip turns the whole piece off.
Screen opens straight to the water
In kiosk mode, a browser shows veritas full screen. If it closes or crashes, it reopens in ten seconds.
Nothing over the glass
Touch screens read a finger through their own glass only. Its black frame sits around the screen, so touch stays exact.
Cables down the drain
Already a hole to the space under the bowl, the drain means no drilling, no cables in sight, and the Mac mini sits where water would go.
