1  v[an]itas
Sink: stainless, freestanding, veritas in its bowl.
Statement
vanitas and veritas are one swap of letters apart. the whole difference is a choice.

A big print pulls you across the room, and you reach the basin already in her posture. The tap gives no water, nothing to wash in but more image.

Vanitas: four hundred years of skulls, dead flowers and soap bubbles saying you will die. Homo bulla, man is a bubble. Mine are AI bubbles, twice over: the soap bubble the Dutch painted for fragility, and the one many of us fear we are standing inside.

The print is my self-portrait, hung as a mirror: your face lands on mine and wears the dissolve. I am not above the slop. Hold the eye on the glass and a small camera lends your face to the next dream, once; the basin dreams you, then lets you go down the drain. The photo is never kept.

I freedive; you cannot hold water. Pilate washed his hands of the truth. This asks the reverse. The veritas is the hand that reaches anyway, and the taste that steers the machine: every dream leans toward eight pictures I chose. In a room of generated everything, that choice is the only ground truth left.

no water in the sink,
so you can’t wash your hands of it.

Its screen boots straight into the water; a power cut is just the tap turned off. On the web, the same water runs as veritas, and a touch in either moves both. On the sink, a QR code opens it: sink == sync: one water.

under the hood

One Apple M5 laptop, 16 GB. Half precision: 2.3 GB of GPU memory, handed back after every dream. Upscaling runs in a process of its own. Only the last 60 frames stay on disk. After a GPU error, it restarts from the last dream while queued frames keep pouring. Code: github.com/kat-titter/vanitas-veritas (spring.py, latent.py, upscale.py, sync.html).

Context — tiat · slop · sink
Made for tiat’s open call Epistemologies of Slop (151 Powell St, SF), a show on AI “slop.” Slop is everything but the kitchen sink, so the work brings the sink. tiat’s basement already keeps “a slop station (an empty room with one metal sink)”, found after the sink was drawn (Hayes, Gazetteer SF, 2025). And on 26 October 2022 Elon Musk carried one into Twitter, “let that sink in” (Feiner, CNBC, 2022).
