vanitas-veritas/davos_2027

veri+as

Working notes for a Kat Titterton and Olive Allen piece for Davos, January 2027. It starts from the sink in Vanitas / Veritas and asks what happens when the water is allowed to move, and when nothing that goes down the drain is allowed to leave.

proposal · 2026-09-29 · a seed, open for edits

00  outline

thesisnothing goes away. it comes back as slop. The sink is a closed loop: what goes down the drain comes back out of the tap, and what keeps coming back grows a culture on the rim. models drink what they pour. so do we. Generative models now train on their own output; so do markets; so do feeds.
visiona sink you cannot empty. a picture you can hold still. The only clear thing in the loop is a held moment, and you have to find where it is clear. two basins. two cities. one loop. A double vanity: what one artist drains, the other pours, San Francisco to New York.
impactthe culture grows on the rim. wipe it; it grows back. Visitors can clear it with a hand and watch it return; nothing they add ever leaves the room. stillness takes two. hold your breath together. Two handles, too far apart for one person.
what it isa screen where the drain should be, under fluted glass, in a steel sink, running live software. eight stills. one card in your pocket: a lenticular that tilts between vanitas and veritas.

01  the piece

open it full size ->  ·  ii: the version that loses ->

ii is the same basin with one change of nature: the still pours in once, and after that the basin drinks only what it has drained. Each pass through the drain returns as a copy of a copy. The decay runs on one clock for everyone: it began on 29 September 2026 and reaches the bottom on 18 January 2027, so whoever opens it sees that day's state (?day=N looks ahead). After the eighth line the drain takes the rest, and what remains is the sign. No ash, no fog, no fringe, no ring of cuts.

A basin seen from above, under fluted glass. Twenty-four stills from the vanitas loop melt toward the drain and come back out of the tap twelve seconds later. Hold, and the picture stills; somewhere in it a window of the glass is clear. Move to it, keep your hand there for one heartbeat, and a line of this outline is read. Wipe the lace on the rim for a while and it reads you its own line. Every hold composes a new still and hides the place somewhere else. Eight finds and the poem gives way to the outline. Round the rim a culture grows: a reaction-diffusion lace that clears where your hand passes and grows back. The ring of cuts spells the poem in binary, and lights an eighth for every line you have read. The pulse is 38 bpm, a freediver's heart on a breath-hold.

02  what style makes sense

The lenticular. The ridged glass in the singulari+i image and the ridged plastic on a 2000s holo trading card are the same optical object. One is a lens you look through; the other is a collectible you tilt. A lenticular shows two pictures from one surface depending on where you stand, which is already the whole premise of vanitas / veritas.

KatOlivewhere they meet
surfacefluted glass, comb-fringed edges, graingloss, vinyl, blister pack, foila fluted lens with holo foil in the highlights
colourbone on deep blackcandy, saturatedfour colours only: ink, bone, ember, ice. Chosen to stay apart for colour-blind viewers
languagecode-poems, equations as versedrops, sets, game rulesa poem that is also the rulebook
objectthe sink, the screen in the drainthe collectible, the toyeight signs, issued as a set, poured through a basin
timeslow, looping, held breaththe drop: scarce, scheduled, hypeda slow loop punctuated by timed drops

Rules I would hold to: signs are drawn with one stroke weight, like pictograms on a 2003 device. Fluid is cel-shaded with an inked contour and one hard highlight, so it reads as cartoon, then given real grain and a soft second highlight so it does not quite. No periodic waves. Colour arrives only in a pour or in the foil.

Third pass, after "weird, mysterious, apocalyptic, shamanistic": the sink becomes a scrying basin. Same lens, same signs, but staged as a rite. The disc is a black sun with a fibrous corona that brightens on the beat. Ash rises. Ember and ice are the only colours, one pour at a time. Fog crosses the rim and the glass is scratched. Space is the other material: the disc is cropped by the edge of the screen and the words sit small in what is left.

Caveat: what I know of Olive's work is public and mostly 2019 to 2022 (Pokémon cards, Furbies, UnBearables, drops, the passport burn). If her current work has moved, this table should move with it.

04  six ways in

adouble vanity

A double vanity is a bathroom unit with two sinks. Two basins, two artists, one loop: what drains from Kat's basin pours from Olive's tap, and back. Can run side by side in one room, or San Francisco to New York over a network with the delay left visible.

Davos reading: two parties who cannot wash their hands without the other receiving it.

bthe set of eight

Eye, drop, bubble, skull, bloom, hand, tap, loop. Eight signs issued as physical lenticular cards that flip vanitas / veritas. The basin is where the set is read aloud. Olive's territory: how a set is released, what is rare, what completing it gets you.

The eight map onto the eight source frames of the existing loop.

cliquidity pool

The basin taken literally. Each visitor adds one drop of pigment. It circulates, dilutes, and never leaves. By Friday the pool is the colour of everyone who came. The colour on the last day is the work.

Davos reading: liquidity, trickle-down, and who gets to wash their hands.

dnull address

Olive's Post-death or The Null Address (2021) takes its title from the address tokens are sent to when they are burned. A drain makes the same promise. Here the promise is broken: whatever is burned into the drain comes back out of the tap.

Mechanic: burn to pour.

ehold

The existing piece pauses when you hold the faucet. Give it two handles, too far apart for one person. The loop only stops when two strangers hold at once, for as long as they can both stand to.

Davos sits at about 1,560 m. Breath is already short.

fsnowmelt

No screen. Real water, a small pump, a fluted acrylic disc over a lit basin, pigment dosed on the heartbeat. The water is snow collected outside the venue on day one and returned on day five.

Hardest to build, easiest to explain.

My recommendation: a + b. The double vanity is the object, the set of eight is the language, and the study above is already most of the screen content. e folds into it for free, since the hold sensor exists.

04b  format

Live software first, one object second, a card in the hand third. The work is a real-time piece that runs in a browser. Everything else is a container for it.

stageformatwhy
teaser, nowThe study as a link, plus a 20 second capture for feeds.Already exists. Costs nothing to send to a curator or a house.
Davos, JanuaryOne basin or one window screen running the live work. A lenticular card, or a newsprint tabloid, handed over on the street. Venue is unknown and the week runs on thirty second encounters and things passed hand to hand. Software fits any wall; a card travels in a pocket.
full exhibit, afterThe double vanity: two basins, fluted discs, the hold handles, the set of eight on the wall.Needs a room, freight and time. Davos is the proof, not the build.
  • Not a video. The loop never repeats, the hold has to be live, and what drains has to come back. A render would be a recording of the piece.
  • Not water, yet. Pumps and pigment are a freight and leak problem in a borrowed space. Keep it for the full exhibit.
  • Not a room-scale projection. That is the Forum's own commissioned territory and budget. A small dense object reads better against it.
  • The AI has to be real. The study is a fluid simulation. For an AI art context the honest options are the diffusion loop poured through the basin (exists), signs dreamed by a model instead of drawn, or an oracle that reads the basin and answers in a line of code-poem. One of these should be load-bearing, not a label.
  • The edition is Olive's call. Card, token, both, or neither. The software does not care.

05  build notes

What the page is, technically. One HTML file, no server, no libraries. WebGL2.

  • Basin: a stable-fluids solver (semi-Lagrangian advection, 36 Jacobi pressure iterations) on a 256² grid with a source at the tap and a sink at the drain, a swirl that reverses each still, and a 1024² dye field. The dye is the picture: it is fed from the current still and carried by the flow.
  • The return: what is going down the drain is sampled every frame into a 12-second ring buffer and poured back in at the tap.
  • Glass: 58 flutes per basin width; each compresses its slice sideways and drags bright edges into a fringe. Holding flattens the glass in proportion to how near you are to the clear place.
  • Stills: 24 frames sampled evenly round the 8:26 loop, centre-cropped to 768² WebP and inlined by build.py. Each hold composes a new one: the current frame, a random partner, each zoomed, turned and mirrored by chance, merged through a noise mask.
  • Lace: Gray-Scott reaction-diffusion on a 4096×256 polar field (holes regime at the rim, mitosis further out), six steps a frame, drawn as contour threads with glints. The pointer clears it; it regrows.
  • The face (ii): once, a line offers let it see you. On a click and the browser's own permission, one mirrored frame is taken, the camera is stopped, the frame is graded like the stills and poured into the loop, where it drains and returns like everything else. It exists only in the loop's ten-second memory on the viewer's machine; nothing is stored or sent, and the next two holds may bring it up clear.
  • The path: every place found is marked and joined to the last. Eight finds make a sign no one else will draw; it is encoded in a link (?p=, 32 hex characters) that can be sent, and a sent path is drawn in gold in the receiver's basin.
  • Ring of cuts: 240 cuts, long for 1 and short for 0, spelling sink=[sync]*n;n->inf;sink=sync.
  • Finish: a soft-focus pass (quarter-resolution blur, lighter inside the basin), desaturation to 58%, one warm tint, grain. Four colours: ink, bone, ember, ice.
  • Render cost: 3 to 9 ms a frame on an M-series laptop at 1.5×2K. Press r to record a 15-second clip.

Toward the installation.

  • The tiat build seals a flat acrylic disc over the basin screen. Swapping it for a fluted or lenticular acrylic sheet gets the lens look optically, with no shader. The on-screen lens in the study is a stand-in for that.
  • The Raspberry Pi, hall sensor and magnet from the build guide carry over. The study is a web page, so the Pi runs a browser in kiosk mode instead of a video player. Worth testing early: a Pi 4 GPU may need the simulation grid reduced.
  • The slop feed mode pours the existing basin loop through the same fluid, so the 8:26 render is reusable as pigment.
  • Shipping a stainless sink to Switzerland is the expensive part. The Pearlhaus joke works just as well bought locally.

06b  art at davos, before us

What the Forum has actually shown, 2023 to 2026, and what it tells us. The programme is run by Joseph Fowler, whose line for it is "art ventures where policy briefs and position papers cannot go".

What repeats. Live data as material. Ecology as the sanctioned subject. AI as a partner, never as a problem. Big, luminous, room-scale. And, once, the opposite: Abramović's bus, small, dark, closed, asking for stillness, which she herself doubted executives would give.

What is missing, which is where we stand. Nothing shown has been about AI's own residue, or about the viewer's part in it. Nothing is allowed to end. The one published criticism of the programme (Right Click Save, 2025) is that art at Davos washes reputations and lets policymakers delegate ethics to artists. A sink you cannot wash your hands in answers that directly.

What to take.

  • Be the shortest ritual in Davos. THE BUS asks twenty minutes. We ask one breath, and ninety seconds to read the whole thing. It fits between sessions, and it leaves something in the pocket.
  • Put it where they wash their hands. The bus was parked outside the Congress Centre; JR pasted on facades. The sink belongs in a restroom of a Promenade house, the one room every delegate enters alone. Art where the policy briefs cannot go.
  • Live data, but ours is a clock. Forestate runs on deforestation; Living Archive on weather. Ours runs on the days until the meeting, and it will have emptied by the time they arrive. That is the only feed it needs.
  • Faces: our own, not borrowed. Anadol used the Yawanawá's drawings; JR used strangers' faces. The faces melting in our basin are the artist's. The viewer's lands on top. No one is witnessed on someone else's behalf.
  • Duration as material. Sunde stood in the sea for a tide. One held breath a day at the sink, in person, at a fixed hour, is a performance the Forum's audience would understand.
  • Let it end. Every work listed runs for the week and loops. Ours is the one that will be over.
