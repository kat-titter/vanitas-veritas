veri+as

The context for veri+as, a work by Kat Titterton, developed with Olive Allen, for Davos 2027: what it says, where it comes from, who makes it, and how a web page and a real sink become one piece. A white paper, in black. That is part of the joke.

black paper · october 2026
In short. A new biology, grown in a sink you cannot wash your hands in. Where the drain should be, AI-made vanitas pictures drift on a random walk, melt, and come back as copies of copies. Hold still and the glass clears on a new form of life: a face with the skull showing through, a skull in flower, bubbles becoming cells. How you come to it decides what grows, and at the end your constellation is read back to you like a mirror, flattering and true. When enough of us hold at once, anywhere, the title turns from v[an]itas to v[er]itas.

01  the point

we need a new biology.

Kat’s first line is the thesis. The piece makes new forms of life out of what keeps draining and coming back, and it stays in the uncanny in-between: between life and death, a face with the skull already showing through; between species, a skull in flower; between matter and life, bubbles that become cells. Nothing is drawn by hand. It emerges, soft and fluid, out of a loop that never empties, and every form is new because every person makes it differently.

you can't wash your hands of it.

  • The loop. Whatever goes down the drain comes back out of the tap. The model drinks what it pours; the feed serves what we fed it. Nothing drains away, and everyone is standing at the same sink.
  • Davos. The one published critique of art at the Forum is that corporations use art to wash their reputations1. A sink you cannot wash your hands in, placed where delegates wash their hands, answers that without a word.
  • The gesture. The only clean act left is to stop. Hold, and the slop goes still and true; let go, and it floods back. Vanitas and veritas are one letter-swap apart, and the whole difference is a choice2.

sink == sync

In 1665 Christiaan Huygens found that two pendulum clocks hung from one beam had fallen into a shared rhythm, swinging in exact opposition, coupled only through the beam3. Three centuries later Yoshiki Kuramoto wrote down how a crowd of clocks, each with its own pace, falls into one rhythm once the coupling between them passes a threshold4. The feed is the beam. It sets everyone moving to one rhythm: smooth, unified, beautiful, the vanitas state. A hold takes one person out of it. Enough holds at the same moment, anywhere, and the rhythm breaks for everyone. That is why the credit line asks for two letters.

Kat’s words. Seven are found in the basin, one at each clear place; the eighth belongs to the eye. Spoken by the sink they turn double, tender and a little frightening at once: waste or treasure, the same ambivalence as the first sink’s print.

under the glass

One HTML page, no framework. The 24 stills are taken from the Vanitas Veritas loop, which was dreamed as a walk through a diffusion model’s latent space; the page walks that space again, at random, one still at a time, so the sequence never repeats. A fluid solver carries the pictures from the tap to the drain; what drains is remembered and poured back, dimmer each pass. A reaction-diffusion culture grows on the rim. The glass is 2,048 coupled clocks4. Holds travel as anonymous events over public Nostr relays: no server, no accounts, no pictures, only where in the basin a hand is held and for how long. The pictures are the stills of the Vanitas Veritas loop2. The same page runs on a wall (?gallery) and in a real sink (?sink=davos), where the faucet is the hold.

03  the study

Every play is also a measurement. On the published page, each play leaves one anonymous record: how long the holds were, how straight the hands went, how much they scrubbed, the shape of the constellation, the names of what grew. No picture, no face, no name, no address. The records sit on public Nostr relays as app data, so anyone can read the whole population, and the patterns in how people play can be found after the fact rather than decided in advance. Nobody is sorted into a type by the piece; the mirror only reads one play back to the one who played it.

For the room, the same records draw everyone’s sky: every constellation made so far, at once, growing denser through January. That map can hang beside the sink in Davos. A small count me out under the piece keeps any play out of the study.

04  where it comes from

  • The genre. Vanitas still lifes, from Leiden in the early 1600s, pile up skulls, cut flowers and soap bubbles to say that everything you want is already going5. Homo bulla: man is a bubble.
  • The loop. Vanitas Veritas (2026): eight pictures grown from Kat’s self-portrait style code, melted into one another with open-source diffusion, an eight-and-a-half-minute loop with no first frame and no last2.
  • The first sink. Designed in 2026 for tiat’s Epistemologies of Slop call in San Francisco: a freestanding stainless sink, a screen where the drain should be, a faucet that pauses the loop instead of running water, and a self-portrait hung as a mirror2. It was not built for that show; it is the object Davos inherits.
  • The web pieces. i and ii, September and October 2026, kept as they were. iii is this one.

05  who makes it

Kat Titterton · San Francisco

Imaging, signal and ML scientist: expansion microscopy at MIT’s Synthetic Neurobiology Group; the Cell Painting screens behind two of the largest such datasets, at Axiom Bio; tau-aggregation screens and microfluidic neuron-culture devices at AbbVie; eight peer-reviewed papers6. Generative artist: Bare Metal, a solo exhibit at Human Tech Week, San Francisco, 20256; Vanitas Veritas, 20262; code-poems as macrofluidic. A freediver: the hold is a breath-hold.

Olive Allen · New York

Among the first artists to release work as NFTs, from 2019. Pop and toy iconography with social commentary; original characters HYPEBIRDS, UnBearables and The Sheeplezzz; Mad Toy Junction, a metaverse gallery and NFT factory; a collaboration on TIME’s first NFT drop; Post-death or The Null Address, described as the first NFT artwork sold at Art Basel7. A solo show, Welcome to the Metaverse, at Postmasters, New York, 20228. In 2022 she burned her Russian passport on video and auctioned it as an NFT for Ukraine relief9.

the union in one image: a double vanity.

Two sinks, two mirrors, two faces. What drains from one pours from the other. Olive’s work has been about release: drops, burns, editions. Kat’s is about what comes back. Together the piece is the whole cycle.

06  davos

The Forum’s arts programme is run by Joseph Fowler, head of arts and culture, who writes that art “ventures into spaces where policy briefs and position papers cannot go”10. In 2026 it held Thijs Biersteker’s Forestate, Marina Abramović’s THE BUS, Ronen Tanchum’s Human Atmospheres, JR’s Wrinkles of the City, and a concert with live AI visuals10.

What has been missing. Nothing shown has been about AI’s own residue, or the viewer’s part in it, and nothing has been allowed to end.

The critique. Right Click Save argued that corporations use art to wash their reputations, and that policymakers lean on artists for ethical work they should face themselves1. The sink takes that literally.

Art about Davos. Thomas Hirschhorn’s Wirtschaftslandschaft Davos (2001) set out to explain what the Forum had done to the town, down to the policing that turns it into a fortress each January12. The Kirchner Museum Davos will show new works made for Davos by Anna Scalfi Eghenter in 202713.

three basins, one loop

The web sink, open to everyone; a real sink in San Francisco or New York; the Davos sink, for one week. All of them run the same page.
