veri+as

black paper · october 2026

If you answered with two letters, this is what they open. If you were sent here, the riddle is still waiting in the piece. This is the context for vanitas_veritas, a work in a sink by Kat Titterton, in conversation with Olive Allen, made toward Davos in January 2027. A white paper, in black.

In short. A new biology, grown in a sink you cannot wash your hands in. Where the drain should be, AI-made vanitas pictures drift, melt and come back as copies of copies. Hold still and the glass clears on a new form of life. How you play decides what grows. Seven finds make your youniverse, named in Latin and read back to you like a horoscope, flattering and true. When enough of us hold at once, anywhere, the title turns from v[an]itas to v[er]itas.

eight minutes to read. longer, if you open the folds. the piece itself: veri+as iii, in a browser. hold still.

01  the thesis

we need a new biology.

Kat Titterton’s first line is the thesis. The piece makes new forms of life out of what keeps draining and coming back, and it stays in the uncanny in-between: between life and death, a face with the skull already showing through; between species, a skull in flower; between matter and life, bubbles that become cells. Nothing is drawn by hand. It emerges, soft and fluid, out of a loop that never empties, and every form comes from how one person grew it.

you can't wash your hands of it.

The loop is literal: whatever goes down the drain comes back out of the tap, the way a model trained on its own output drinks what it pours. Nothing drains away, and everyone stands at the same sink. At Davos the line has a second edge. A published critique of art at the Forum is that corporations use it to wash their reputations1. A sink you cannot wash your hands in, placed where people wash their hands, answers that without a word.

sink == sync

In 1665 Christiaan Huygens saw two clocks hung from one beam fall into a shared rhythm, swinging in opposition2; Yoshiki Kuramoto later showed how a crowd of clocks falls into one rhythm once the coupling between them passes a threshold3. The feed is the beam, and its smooth, shared rhythm is the vanitas state. A hold takes one person out of it. Enough holds at the same moment, anywhere, and the rhythm breaks for everyone. The title turns on that: vanitas and veritas, one swap of letters apart4.

the eight lines

resistance is futile.

the spotlight.

Kat’s words, and where the hint poem starts. At each find the piece writes a line of its own from what grew there, and lets one of hers through now and then, in its place. The later lines lean toward the answer. Spoken by a sink they turn double, tender and a little frightening at once.

02  the piece, as it stands

veri+as iii is one web page, open to anyone: a dark basin seen from above, under fluted glass, ringed by a living culture. It is born from nothing each time it opens, explains nothing about itself, and is better found than read. Four things, for the record:

holding still
The fluted glass flattens and the picture beneath comes clear. Scrubbing only spreads it, and the hand comes away gold. A tap clears the glass for a heartbeat; a star glints where one is hidden; the first hold finds its place under the hand.
the hidden stars
Seven places where the glass goes fully clear. Each keeps a new form of life, grown from two of the loop’s pictures and named like a species, Calvaria florens, Homo mortalis, and writes two lines of the program that is your play: a line of poem over a line of code.
holding together
Every hold, anywhere, is a ring in everyone’s basin; and every day at noon, Davos time, everyone holds. In October one long breath can turn the title alone. By January it takes more than one of us.
youniverses
Seven stars make a youniverse: one person’s universe, grown from how they played. Yours, with the ones sent to you, are your weverse. Everyone’s, on one live map, are the macroverse.
the longer account

What grows. Each form of life is grown from two of the 32 pictures the loop was dreamed through (Kat’s eight, and three dreamed between each pair), or from one of them and you. Its line of code takes its shape from what happened and names how the two were joined: if held > 4: life += [graft(sleeper, froth)]. Over it, a line of the hint poem. The joins are the thesis as verbs: haunt, the living through the dead; graft, across species; culture, grown from cells; merge, two as one. Which join wins comes from how the hand arrived: still or moving, scrubbing, or beside someone else. Any form can be kept, as a card.

the program
Beside the basin runs a program, in Python, written by your play. It opens as a primer, each line a plain comment over its code, the human over the machine: # what drains away comes back / sink = drain(sink). Then it turns into the first of two poems: each find adds a line of the hint poem over its line of code. The function’s last line is there from the start: return youniverse(me + you). Answered, or ended, it reads return youniverse(we).
the culture you push
A culture grows on the rim from two numbers5, how fast it is fed and how fast it dies. How you play pushes them: stillness joins it into a net, moving hands grow loops, scrubbing breaks it into foam, wiping makes it divide and grow back, holding with others sets it into a lattice. You need not read the numbers: its threads turn red when it is underfed, gold in the middle, white when fed. A hand can also turn them directly. By the end it is a culture your hand has shaped, and it stays with your youniverse: on the device, and in the study record.
letting it see you
One frame from your camera, if you allow it, kept in the page; the camera is off again as soon as the frame is taken. Nothing looks for the face: the frame sits in a fixed oval in the middle of what grows. From then on you are the centre of your youniverse, the clear places come easier, and most of what you grow has your face in it, the bone of the dream showing through. Its genus is Ipse: the self.
the folded sky
Your seven stars, the first found furthest away, the last nearest, round a black hole: a shadow, a hairline ring of light, a disk brighter on the side turning toward you, after the first image of M87*6 and the lensed disk made for Interstellar7. It lights as the sky fills and once it has seen you; the picture near it bends and reddens as it falls in. When the sky is whole it lifts off the glass into one folded shape, and turns.
the name
Each youniverse is named in Latin, as code: persona_videns, the mask that sees. The name joins the thing in the form of life you stayed with and what was most your own in how you played. There are 4,096 names, none repeated on this device, nor anywhere the public record reaches.
the reading
The second poem is the ending. It reads you back in pairs: a line of poem, kind and a little obscure, broad enough to see yourself in, and under it a line of code, literal, with the play’s own numbers in it: while held < 14.3: stay(). Vanitas and veritas at once. What you are like comes only from what you did; the moment you came quietly chooses which lines are said, and is never named. Some days, a line comes from the day itself. A light play reads three pairs; a deep one, up to ten.
sharing a youniverse
A link of 52 characters carries a youniverse, never a picture: to X, Bluesky, Threads or LinkedIn, or, with its card, through a phone’s own share sheet; the card can be saved and the link copied. Whoever opens it finds your sky under theirs, and can grow your forms of life again, with their own face wherever yours was. It joins their weverse.
the macroverse
Every finished youniverse, everyone’s, on one map that builds itself live as the records arrive. Each is placed by how it was played, beside the ones played most alike, so clusters and filaments form on their own, after the fact. Your own are gold, joined in the order you grew them; your weverse, the friends whose youniverses you have opened, in white. Choose any one to see its neighbours, or open it in your own glass. A macroverse in three dimensions is in progress (07).

Under the glass. One HTML page, no framework, no server of its own, no accounts. A stable-fluids solver8 carries the pictures from the tap to the drain; what drains is remembered for ten seconds and poured back, dimmer each pass: copies of copies. The page walks among the pictures at random, as the loop once walked a diffusion model’s latent space4. The glass is 2,048 coupled clocks3; the culture is a Gray-Scott reaction-diffusion system5 grown round the rim. One clock runs from launch to the first day of Davos: each day a little less is poured, and the clocks are coupled tighter. Holds travel as ephemeral events over three public Nostr relays, open servers anyone can read. The same page runs on a wall (?gallery) and in a real sink (?sink=davos); without the relays it holds alone.

mirror.learn(you) # the glass is learning your hand

03  the sink

The web page is the invitation; the sink is the work. As designed: a freestanding vanity, about 76 by 64 cm, with a steel basin and a screen under round fluted acrylic where the drain should be. The faucet runs no water: its lever is the hold, and the loop stops. Above it hangs a self-portrait, as a mirror. It would run the same page as the web, and while someone holds its faucet, every connected browser sees a gold ring at the drain.

Status: designed in 2026 for tiat’s Epistemologies of Slop open call in San Francisco4; it has not yet been built. It is the object proposed for Davos.

how it works, and other forms

The lever moves a magnet past a sensor; a small board turns that into the hold. Offline, the lever still quiets its own basin. Online, its hold joins everyone’s. It stores nothing on the device; like the web page, each play leaves one anonymous record. A little after each ending it fills again for the next person.

  • A wall. The same page in its wall mode: the basin centred, the title only, a pad or a button for the hold.
  • The cards. Lenticular cards, vanitas at one tilt and veritas at the other, each carrying the way to the sink. Proposed.
  • The double vanity. Two sinks, side by side or in two cities. See 07.

Texts, formats and a technical rider for hosts and juries are in the pitch kit.

04  what it keeps

Every play on the published page is also a measurement. It leaves one anonymous record: counts of what the hands did, the shape of the constellation and the code that regrows it (shapes and recipes, no picture), its culture’s two numbers (feed and kill), the names of what grew and of the youniverse, how many youniverses this device has grown, whether you came from a shared one, which form of the page it ran in (web, wall or sink), the day and the local hour (which, with the record’s timestamp, shows your time zone), and whether the screen was small. No picture and no face (only how many forms had you in them), no personal name, no address, no account. The records sit on public Nostr relays as app data9, each signed with a throwaway key, so anyone can read the whole population. Patterns are found after the fact, never decided in advance.

To stay out of the count, add ?nostudy to the address, or use a browser that sends Global Privacy Control10, the browser’s do-not-track signal; either is honoured without asking. Only the published page counts.

The page’s host, the relays, and the server the page loads its relay library from are run by others. Like any server a browser reaches, they see a connection’s network address; the events and records themselves carry none.

Proposed: faces in the macroverse. Only by opt-in, as a step of its own: the dreamed picture only, never the camera’s frame; adults, alone in the frame; a separate yes for a projection at Davos; a code to take it down; kept privately in the EU, seen by Kat before it appears anywhere, and gone on a set date. A dreamed face can still be recognised, and the consent will say so. Nothing will measure the face: you frame yourself, as in a mirror. It is to be reviewed under Swiss and EU data-protection law before it opens. Until then, a face leaves the device only on a card you send.

05  lineage

  • The genre. Vanitas still lifes, from Leiden in the early 1600s, pile up skulls, cut flowers and soap bubbles to say that everything you want is already going11.
  • The loop. Vanitas Veritas (2026): eight pictures grown from Kat’s self-portrait style code, melted into one another with open-source diffusion, an eight-and-a-half-minute loop with no first frame and no last4.
  • The web pieces. i and ii, September and October 2026, kept as they were. iii is the one live now.
the science underneath

Clocks that fall into step2,3, patterns that grow out of two numbers5, light bent round a black hole6,7: each says that form emerges, unasked, from simple rules. The eye behind the piece is a neuroscientist’s: cultures, specimens, Latin binomials, living things watched under glass.

06  davos

The World Economic Forum’s Annual Meeting returns to Davos from 18 to 22 January 202712. Its arts programme is run by Joseph Fowler, who writes that art “ventures into spaces where policy briefs and position papers cannot go”13. In 2026 it held Marina Abramović’s THE BUS and Thijs Biersteker’s Forestate, among others.

vanitas_veritas is made for the other register: one basin, proposed for the room people enter alone, and one breath, the shortest ritual in Davos. We have found nothing in the programme about AI’s own residue, and nothing made to run out.

the critique, and art about Davos

The 2026 programme also held Ronen Tanchum’s Human Atmospheres, JR’s Wrinkles of the City and a concert with live AI visuals13. THE BUS took twelve people at a time for 15 to 20 minutes, devices locked away, so that “decision makers can join together in silence”14. Right Click Save argued that corporations use art to wash their reputations, and that policymakers lean on artists for ethical work they should face themselves1. Thomas Hirschhorn’s Wirtschaftslandschaft Davos (2001) set out to explain what the Forum had done to the town, down to the policing that turns it into a fortress each January15. The Kirchner Museum Davos will show new works made for Davos by Anna Scalfi Eghenter in 202716.

vanitas_veritas is an independent work. It is not part of the Forum’s programme, and nothing here is endorsed by the World Economic Forum.

  • birth another, made literal: the finished youniverse falls into its black hole and the next is born out of it, with its parent’s culture, changed a little. Lineages in the macroverse. After Lee Smolin, for whom each black hole births a universe, its laws a little changed17.

08  who is behind it

The riddle was a credit line. macrofluidic is the name Kat Titterton signs her code-poems with; one was signed macrofluidic × midjourney. This one is signed with everyone who holds. Kat is in conversation with Olive Allen about the work; Olive’s part in it is not yet set.

Kat Titterton · San Francisco

Generative artist: Bare Metal, a solo generative-art exhibit at Human Tech Week, San Francisco, 202520; Vanitas Veritas, 20264; code-poems as macrofluidic. Also a scientist in imaging, signal and machine learning, trained in cellular neuroscience and mathematical systems biology: expansion microscopy at MIT’s Synthetic Neurobiology Group; the Cell Painting screens behind two of the largest such datasets, at Axiom Bio; tau-aggregation screens and microfluidic neuron-culture devices at AbbVie; eight peer-reviewed papers20. A freediver: the hold is a breath-hold.

Olive Allen · New York

Releasing work as NFTs since 201921. Pop and toy iconography with social commentary; original characters HYPEBIRDS, UnBearables and The Sheeplezzz; a collaboration on TIME’s first NFT drop; Post-death or The Null Address, described as the first NFT artwork sold at Art Basel21. A solo show, Welcome to the Metaverse, at Postmasters, New York, 202222. In 2022 she burned her Russian passport on video and auctioned it as an NFT for Ukraine relief23.

proposed, part two, in one image: a double vanity.

Two sinks, two mirrors, two faces. What drains from one pours from the other. Olive’s work has been about release: drops, burns, editions. Kat’s is about what comes back. Together they would close the loop.
