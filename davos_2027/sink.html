01  the well

drag to turn

a basalt column, as it cooled and fractured. seven faces, left as they came out of the ground, about 57 cm across.

88 cm to the rim. the top is the one worked face: sawn flat, honed matte, never polished.

in the top, a round hole, 28 cm across. the piece’s disc fills it under glass set flush with the stone. where the drain should be, light.

at the back, where a tap would stand, a brass lever. raw brass, so it darkens where hands hold it.

nothing else. no label, no bezel, no cable in sight, no legs. the screen and the computer are inside the stone.

the stone floats 3 cm over a round black steel plinth set about 6 cm inside its faces. a warm hairline of light on the floor is the only trace of a fixture.

where the form comes from
  • a stone basin before the tea house. you crouch, and the bowl’s mouth is the message: at ryōan-ji its square opening completes four characters read as “i only know plenty”. tsukubai, checked 25 october 2026.
  • basalt cut by machine, set with brass. formafantasma, de natura fossilium, milan, april 2014.
  • a stone column with a stone control, after the baptismal font. giorgio rava for antoniolupi, anima liquida, 2021.
  • furniture as one brutal, elegant block of stone. rick owens: furniture, moca, 2016 to 2017.
  • an aperture with light behind it and no fixture in sight. james turrell, as seen below, his hundredth skyspace, aarhus, june 2026.
  • a ship of one material, no gadgets, no buttons, a shaft like a well. arrival, 2016, production design by patrice vermette.
  • the slab painted black after the glassy prop disappointed kubrick. the monolith, 2001.

the lesson across them: the opposite of toy-like is not shiny. it is heavy, quiet, and does one thing.

two other forms, kept
  • the dark mirror. an l of black stone: a standing slab, polished on its face, with a block cantilevered from it at rim height. you stand at the block and your own face appears, dim, in the slab beyond the basin: the vanitas mirror with no portrait. the hold is a brass plate you rest a hand on. heavier, jointed, and the right idea for a second basin elsewhere.
  • the font. a cast black mass with crushed basalt in it and a round display 60 cm across, so the whole top is a disc of light and the page is seen whole. the hold is a stone you press. concrete has to convince, and the round display has to exist.

02  at the stone

in the stone, the page would show the disc only, no words beside it.

  1. you come up to it. the basin is already alive, its pictures melting toward the drain.
  2. you pull the lever and keep it there. the basin picks the place, and the glass clears there.
  3. in every browser open on the piece, a gold ring circles the drain, and one line appears: someone is holding the sink in san francisco. a hold on a phone, anywhere, is a pale ring in the stone.
  4. the light that runs down the glass in step is the beam. hold long enough, or with enough hands at once, anywhere, and it comes apart: in every browser the title turns from v[an]i+as to v[er]i+as, vanity to truth.
  5. seven places clear. each keeps a new form of life. together they are your youniverse.
  6. your youniverse lifts away, and 75 seconds later the basin fills again for the next person.
  7. on a wall beside it, if the room allows: the macroverse, every youniverse on one live map. yours lands there when it ends.

the web invites and maps. the stone is the place.

04  drawings

for whoever builds it.

stone and glass brass, light, and the dimensions, in mm hidden below
the full spec
form
a vertical prism, as the rock fractured: a seven-sided plan inside a 60 cm circle, faces as quarried, grey where cut. the column is cut once, 12 cm from the top. the cap drops over a steel cradle and the two read as one stone with a hairline seam.
the basin
a 24 in open-frame panel lies flat under the cap, 10 mm below the glass. the round hole shows 28 cm of it and the stone masks the rest. the page centres its disc at 0.45 of the panel’s short side: a disc 26.6 cm across, with the culture running out to the stone. the glass is 30 cm across, 8 mm, low-iron so the black stays black, tempered, polished edge, micro-ribbed along the lever’s axis so the ribs sit in step with the page’s own flutes. it sits flush in a rebate on black silicone. cleaning is one wipe.
the hold
a brass bar, 22 mm across and 28 cm long, rising from a 6 cm brass hub set flush in the top, 7 cm in from the back rim, leaning 10 degrees away at rest. pull it 20 degrees toward you and hold. a magnet in the hub passes a sensor; a spring returns the bar when you let go. a tap lever pulled with nothing coming out: the whole piece in one gesture.
materials
basalt, one natural column, top honed to 400 grit. raw brass for whatever a hand touches. blackened steel, waxed, for the plinth, the cradle and the drawer, all in shadow. low-iron micro-ribbed tempered glass. a matte open-frame panel. a small fanless computer. no acrylic anywhere.
light
the basin is the only light on the object. the panel sits 10 mm under the glass, capped near 250 nit, so ink stays ink in a dark room; the glass edge catches it and the honed top shows a faint ring round the hole. a 2200 K line in the shadow gap faces the floor and draws the gold hairline. the room at 5 to 10 lux, dark walls and floor where the venue allows. nothing is aimed at the piece. when someone holds, here or anywhere, only the basin changes.
the cable
one mains lead leaves the back of the plinth at floor level, under a cord cover painted the floor colour, to one outlet. no plumbing, no wall.
inside the stone

everything that is not stone, brass or glass is inside the stone.

  • a small fanless computer in the plinth’s drawer, running the page full screen in a kiosk browser from power-on. no keyboard, no mouse, the cursor hidden.
  • a flat screen, open frame, matte, about 24 in, lying under the cap on a steel cradle, with its driver board beside it. brightness capped near 250 nit.
  • one cable from the computer up the bore to the screen.
  • a small magnet in the lever’s hub and a hall sensor in a pocket beside it. no contact, no bounce, nothing to wear.
  • a sensor board that acts as a keyboard. while the magnet is near it holds the space key, which is the hold in the page’s sink mode; let go and it releases.
  • one power supply in the drawer. one mains lead out of the back at floor level.
  • a warm led line in the shadow gap, with its driver, in the plinth.
  • a sachet of silica gel in the sealed bay under the glass.

if the sensor fails, the loop keeps running. nothing goes black.

05  to stand it

what a room needs, and what it costs.

  • a room you can darken to 5 to 10 lux, with nothing aimed at the stone.
  • a ground floor: about 270 kg, about 2 t per m² on the plinth. an upper floor needs a check.
  • one outlet. wifi or ethernet for the shared holds; without it the basin holds alone.
  • no staff. it starts itself and comes back after a power cut. to test before any room: two days running, the plug pulled ten times.
  • it keeps nothing and signs nothing. each play leaves one anonymous record, as on the web.
  • riggers with a pallet jack to place it. two people with suction cups lift the cap for service.
  • about a season from the first order, if the shops are free. a guess.

who makes what
  1. a columnar-basalt yard in the pacific northwest supplies the column cut to length and ships it by freight. if it will, the horizontal cut and the bore too.
  2. a bay area stone yard with a bridge saw and a core rig does the rest of the stone: the cut, the bore, the cap pocket, the hole and its rebate, the hub pocket, the honed top.
  3. a metal shop makes the plinth with its drawer, the cradle, the brass hub and bar, and drills the sensor pocket to a template. blackened and waxed.
  4. a glass shop cuts, tempers and edge-polishes the low-iron ribbed disc.
  5. kat does the panel and its board, the computer, the sensor board, the page in sink mode, and the burn-in.
