01  texts to paste

titleveri+as
one lineA sink you can't wash your hands in.
25 wordsA sink you can't wash your hands in. Hold still and the slop goes clear; hold together, anywhere, and the whole basin turns true.
50 wordsveri+as is a sink you can't wash your hands in. Where the drain should be, a screen loops AI-made vanitas pictures that drain and return as copies of copies. Hold still and the glass clears on a true picture. When enough people hold at once, on the web or at the sink, the title turns from vanitas to veritas.
150 wordsveri+as restages the vanitas still life for the age of the feed. A real sink stands where people wash their hands; a screen sits where the drain should be. In it, faces, skulls and flowers made with AI melt toward the drain and come back from the tap as copies of copies, the way a model trained on its own output does. The faucet gives no water. Holding it stills the loop and clears the fluted glass over a picture that is, for a moment, true. The glass is a field of coupled clocks that falls into one seductive rhythm on its own; each hold takes someone out of it. When enough people hold at once, at the sink or on the open web version, the rhythm breaks for everyone and the title turns from v[an]itas to v[er]itas. Every day at noon in Davos time, everyone holds together.
statement, about 200 wordsVanitas paintings piled up beautiful things to say they were already going. My pictures are AI bubbles, and I mean it twice: the soap bubble the Dutch painted for fragility, and the speculative one many of us fear we are standing in. The slop here is the pretty kind, over-rendered until it squeaks, and it does what generative models now do: whatever drains comes back, a copy of a copy. You cannot wash your hands of it. The faucet is the off switch. I freedive, and holding it is the same as holding a breath underwater: stillness on purpose, against everything in you that wants to let go. Hold, and the picture is true for as long as you can stand to stay. Let go, and it floods back. The piece adds one more thing the Dutch did not have: other people. Everyone holding, anywhere, at the same moment, loosens the rhythm the feed keeps us all in. Huygens saw two clocks on one beam fall into step in 1665; the feed is our beam. veri+as asks how many of us it takes to stop together.

The statement is drafted from Kat’s own TIAT statement; edit it in your voice before sending.

02  three parts

whenwhat
previewnow to December 2026The web basin, open to everyone, with a shared hold at noon in Davos every day. First showings: a salon, a window, a projection.
part one18 to 22 January 2027One real sink in Davos, in the room people enter alone1. The web and the sink hold together at noon. On the 22nd the loop runs out.
part twosummer and autumn 2027The double vanity: two sinks, two artists. What drains from one pours from the other. Shown where it can stay.

Each part stands alone, and each one can be pitched alone: the web piece to an online or festival call, a single sink to an exhibition, the double vanity to a museum.

06  bios

Kat Titterton, about 80 wordsKat Titterton is a San Francisco artist and imaging scientist. She has built expansion-microscopy pipelines at MIT, microfluidic neuron-culture devices at AbbVie, and the Cell Painting screens behind two of the largest such datasets at Axiom Bio. As macrofluidic she makes generative work about what images do once machines make them: Bare Metal (Human Tech Week, San Francisco, 2025) and Vanitas Veritas (2026). She is a freediver, which is where the hold comes from.
