150 wordsvanitas_veritas restages the vanitas still life as a new biology. A sink, designed to stand where people wash their hands, has a screen where the drain should be; the same basin is open to anyone in a browser. In it, faces, skulls and flowers made with AI drift on a random walk, melt toward the drain and come back from the tap as copies of copies, the way a model trained on its own output does. Hold still and the fluted glass clears on a new form of life: a face with the skull showing through, a skull in flower, bubbles becoming cells. Each is named like a species, and a culture on the rim grows from how you play. Seven finds make a youniverse, read back to you as a poem written in Python, flattering and true. When enough people hold at once, the title turns from v[an]itas to v[er]itas.
statement, about 200 wordsVanitas paintings piled up beautiful things to say they were already going. My pictures are AI bubbles, and I mean it twice: the soap bubble the Dutch painted for fragility, and the speculative one many of us fear we are standing in. The slop here is the pretty kind, over-rendered until it squeaks, and it does what generative models now do: whatever drains comes back, a copy of a copy. You cannot wash your hands of it. The faucet is the off switch. I freedive, and holding it is the same as holding a breath underwater: stillness on purpose, against everything in you that wants to let go. Hold, and the picture is true for as long as you can stand to stay. Let go, and it floods back. Hold, and what comes clear is something new: not quite alive, not quite dead, not quite one species. The piece adds one more thing the Dutch did not have: other people. Everyone holding, anywhere, at the same moment, loosens the rhythm the feed keeps us all in. Huygens saw two clocks on one beam fall into step in 1665; the feed is our beam. vanitas_veritas asks how many of us it takes to stop together.

02  the piece

what it is
the programA primer, then a poem, written in Python. Each opening line has a plain-English comment over its code, and each find adds a line. return youniverse(me + you) turns to return youniverse(we) once the riddle is answered or the play ends.
the cultureA Gray-Scott slime-mould culture on the rim, pushed by how you play: still, moving, scrubbing, wiping, holding with others. Each youniverse ends with its own.
the cameraOptional, on the web only: one frame, kept on the device and never sent, puts your face at the centre of what grows.
the readingAt the end, lines of poem, kind and a little obscure, each over a line of code with the play’s own numbers in it, such as while held < 14.3: stay(). A light play reads short and a deep one long, 3 to 10 pairs. The youniverse is named in Latin, as code.
the namesyouniverse, one person’s universe; weverse, you and your friends; macroverse, everyone’s. In progress: the macroverse as a live 3D map, each youniverse placed by how alike it was played.
sharingOne link carries a youniverse in 52 characters, never a picture: to X, Bluesky, Threads or LinkedIn, or with its card through the phone’s own share sheet.
nextProposed: the drain grows into a black hole as stars are found, of a kind set by the play. Birth another: a new youniverse born out of the old one’s black hole, after Smolin’s fecund universes1, inheriting its culture, so lineages form in the macroverse. Faces in the macroverse, opt-in only: the dreamed image, never the raw frame, with its own consent step, and every image approved by Kat.

03  seasons

One work in two places. The web is the invitation and the map; the sink is the place; a hold at either would be felt at both, over the relays.

whenwhat
the webnow, liveThe web basin, open to everyone, with a shared hold every day at noon, central European time. Each day a little less pours, and it takes more of us holding at once to turn the title.
the sink2027, proposedA first sink in San Francisco, in the room people enter alone, running the same page and holding with the web at noon. No venue is confirmed. Then wherever an open call takes it.
two basinslater, proposedA second sink elsewhere, with one loop between the two: what drains from one pours from the other’s tap.

Each also shows alone: the web piece online or at a festival, one sink in an exhibition, two basins in a museum.

06  bio

Kat Titterton, about 80 wordsKat Titterton is a San Francisco artist and imaging scientist. She ran and tuned expansion-microscopy pipelines at MIT, designed microfluidic neuron cultures at AbbVie, and oversaw the Cell Painting screens behind two of the largest such datasets at Axiom Bio. As macrofluidic she makes generative work about what images do once machines make them: Bare Metal (Human Tech Week, San Francisco, 2025) and vanitas_veritas (2026, ongoing). She is a freediver, which is where the hold comes from.
